# AI Use Disclosure

## Where and how AI was used
- Used AI to scaffold the repository structure and draft the Python workflow.
- Used AI to map the paper's table columns to the variables in `public.csv`.
- Used AI to draft the README, `skills/SKILL.md`, and the report template.
- Used AI to suggest robustness checks for the independent extension.

## How AI-generated code, analysis, and writing were checked
- All generated Python files were executed with `python code/run_all.py`.
- The constructed FTE variable was checked against the paper's definition: full-time workers including managers plus 0.5 times part-time workers.
- The treatment of closed stores was checked against the paper: permanently closed stores have Wave-2 employment set to zero; temporarily closed stores are treated as missing in the baseline and set to zero in a robustness row.
- Table 4 sample restrictions were checked against the paper: stores with employment and starting-wage data in both waves, giving approximately 351 stores.
- The Figure 1 wage bins and the state legend were checked against the paper's figure.
- All numeric outputs are generated by code, not typed manually.

## Important AI errors or suggestions that were corrected or rejected
- AI initially suggested defining FTE as `EMPFT + 0.5 * EMPPT` and excluding managers. This was rejected because the paper includes managers in full-time-equivalent employment. The corrected definition is `EMPFT + NMGRS + 0.5 * EMPPT`.
- AI initially suggested treating all `STATUS2 > 1` as missing. This was rejected because permanent closures (`STATUS2 = 3`) must have Wave-2 employment set to zero, while temporary closures (`STATUS2 = 2, 4, 5`) are treated as missing in the baseline.
- AI suggested clustering standard errors at the store level. This was not used in the baseline because the paper reports conventional OLS standard errors.
